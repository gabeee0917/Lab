Theory in 2 minutesDegrees, chords and what each chord is for. Tap the buttons to hear each idea.
1

Notes have numbers

In any key, the seven notes are numbered 1 to 7. 1 is home. In C major: C is 1, D is 2, E is 3 and so on. A ♭ (flat) means one fret lower: ♭3 is the "minor" 3.

2

Chords are stacked numbers

Take a note, skip one, take the next: 1 + 3 + 5 is a triad, the basic chord. Built on 1 you get C major; built on 6 (A + C + E) you get A minor. Add the 7 for a richer sound.

3

Every chord has a job

Home chords feel settled (1, 6). Away chords open things up (4, 2). Tension chords want to go home (5, 7). Most songs go home → away → tension → home.

4

One chord, many shapes

A shape (or voicing) is one way to play a chord on the neck. Same notes, different strings and frets: some easy, some colourful, some with ringing open strings.

    How it picks the next chord

    It works out your key from the chords you play, then knows what each degree usually does: 5 pulls to 1, 2 sets up 5, 6 drops to 4. On top of that it mixes in other families of move, weighted by the style you choose:

    • In the key: the functional moves songs use most.
    • Smooth voice leading: chords that keep notes and move the rest by a step.
    • Borrowed: ♭7, ♭6, ♭3 and the minor 4 from the parallel minor (or the major 4 and 5 in a minor key).
    • Sus & resolve: a suspension, then where it falls.
    • Secondary 5: the 5 of another chord, then that chord.
    • Jazz: 2–5–1s, tritone subs, passing diminished, backdoor ♭7.
    • Cinematic: major chords a 3rd away with one note held.

    Some moves are short ideas over 2–3 chords (♭6 → ♭7 → 1, sus4 → resolve). It plays those out before choosing again, and steers toward a 5 or a 1 at the end of each phrase, the way real progressions breathe.

    How chord melody works

    Melody on top. The tune is always the highest note. Pick it slightly harder than the rest.

    Not every note needs a chord. Put chords on strong beats and long notes; the notes in between are passing notes over the held chord.

    Match the texture to the note length. Full grips on long notes, shells or dyads on quick ones. Auto does this for you.

    Shells (root + 3 or 7 + melody) are the backbone of chord melody: the root says where you are, the 3/7 says what kind of chord it is.

    Fragments drop the root and keep the colour, which is great over a bass player or when you're moving fast.

    Swap one thing at a time. Change one chord or one grip, play it, then decide.
